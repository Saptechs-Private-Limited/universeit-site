    Where the data stops

    Data isolation is enforced by tenant, and again by campus.

    Every institution gets its own HANA container. Inside it, every read and every write is filtered again by branch scope, driven by the model rather than by handler code, and fail-closed: a user with no resolvable scope sees nothing, not everything. A row marked GLOBAL is inherited read-only by every campus; a campus that needs a variant authors its own.

    ◫

    One container per tenant

    Not a shared table with a tenant column. Each subscribing institution is deployed into its own HDI container, so the isolation is physical, not a WHERE clause someone can forget.

    Timetables

    One weekly shape, authored once. Every section's own timetable, editable forever after.

    A template is the shape of the week and nothing else — the periods and breaks of each weekday, with their times. No subjects, no teachers. A pre-primary day and a B.Tech day are different shapes, and the catalogue holds both; a campus picks one per section during year planning, and the periods are copied in as fresh, editable blocks on that section's timetable. Copied, not linked — so retiring a template never disturbs a section already running on it.

    ▤

    Add a period to five days at once

    "Period 1, 09:00–09:45, Monday to Friday" is one action, not five, because a week is usually the same shape throughout. It is a convenience and not a constraint: every period and every break carries its own day and its own start and end, so one day can differ from the rest whenever it needs to.

    Multi-day entry · per-day times
    ⧉

    Play group to postgraduate, one model

    A rest hour, a double-period laboratory and a flat school week are all just blocks on a day. Sections, teacher assignment and exam scheduling never fork by institution type, so nothing has to be rebuilt when a trust opens a pre-primary wing.

    Flat · stepped · pre-primary · same model
    ⚠

    Clashes are checked, not solved

    Overlapping blocks are caught when you save, a teacher's assignment carries its periods per week, and a room booked twice is the same question the space register already answers for clubs, meetings and exams. There is no solver that generates a clash-free week for you — templates are authored, not computed. Said plainly because it is the honest limit.

    How it holds together

    One read surface. Two kinds of writer.

    There is no single table holding every dated thing. A fact that has a real home keeps it, and the calendar reflects it — projected on read, never copied. So an exam rescheduled in the Exams app is already right here, because there was never a second copy to drift.

    Stored here

    Facts that are purely a date, an audience and a scope — they have no richer home, so the calendar is their home. Terms, holidays, events, and recurring weekly-offs expanded into holidays as they are read.

    Term boundary · public holiday · sports day · every second Saturday

    Reflected, never copied

    Facts that own a lifecycle, an approval and a detail model of their own. They stay where they live and appear here read-only; clicking one takes you to the app that owns it rather than editing a duplicate.

    Exam timetable → Exams · club event → Clubs

    Bus route schedules and statutory due-date trackers are deliberately left out. They are recurring operational schedules with their own semantics, not point events on a timeline — folding them in would make both worse.

    First contact

    The first thing a parent ever sees is one file that simply works.

    They open it from an SMS, on a basic Android or an iPhone, often on a poor connection. They are not looking for a colourful page — they want the thing to work, first time, without installing anything. So it is one HTML file: no framework, no web fonts, no CDN, nothing to download. It loads, it does its job, and it gets out of the way.

    The decisions behind it
    Identity

    One person, many schools, many hats.

    A teacher is very often also a parent. A parent very often has children at two institutions. A login is not an account at a school — it is one human, to whom schools attach relationships. Getting that distinction wrong is how one school locking an account locks the same person out of another one, and it is why the model below exists.

    The person

    Global · one per mobile number
    Holds
    The sign-in identity, the recovery email, and whether the human is active at all.
    Scope
    Every institution, forever.
    Changed by
    The person, by proving they hold their own channels.

    The relationship

    Local · inside one institution's own database
    Holds
    Which people this login speaks for here, and whether it may act here at all.
    Scope
    This school only. School A cannot see School B's relationships.
    Changed by
    That school's administrator, and nobody else.

    A fact about the person is global, and only the person may change it. A fact about the relationship is local, and only that school may change it.

    A local decision must never write a global fact.

    Credentials are never in the database

    A provider record holds a pointer to the secret and the timestamp it was set — never the secret. The value itself lives in a credential store, namespaced per institution, so one campus's gateway key is not readable from another's.

    Per-tenant credential store
    ⇄

    Failover, and a log per recipient

    More than one provider can serve a channel, chosen by priority and validity window, so an expiring contract is a date rather than an outage. Every send is logged per recipient with the provider's own message id, and a failed run tells the office to send it by hand.

    Provider failover · per-recipient delivery log

    Record-keeping posture

    Designed for the audit you will eventually be asked to survive.

    Accreditation visits, statutory inspections and data-protection notices all ask the same question: can you show what happened, and who saw what? That answer is built into the data model, not bolted on as a log file.

    👁

    Reads are logged, not just writes

    When a member of staff opens a student's personal data, the access is recorded with a stated reason. Most systems can tell you who changed a mark. This one can tell you who looked.

    PII access trail · append-only
    ⊘

    Nothing is ever hard-deleted

    Deletion is a platform-wide impossibility, not a policy people remember. Record-count snapshots run daily and drive a data-loss watchdog that notices when a table shrinks.
